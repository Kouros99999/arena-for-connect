# Jurisdiction Profile: City of Laguna Niguel, CA (Orange County)

Date: 2026-10-17
Confidence: medium. The city website (cityoflagunaniguel.org) is blocked from this research environment, so handout contents come from search snippets and secondary guides. Items marked [verify] need confirmation against the live handout or a call to the Building Division.

## Who you deal with

- Community Development Department, 30111 Crown Valley Pkwy, Laguna Niguel, CA 92677
- Building Division: (949) 362-4360. Planning Division handles zoning/setback/use permit questions.
- Outside agencies that may need clearance: Orange County Fire Authority (OCFA), Moulton Niguel Water District, Capistrano Unified School District (school fees on additions above a size threshold [verify, typically 500 sf]).
- HOAs: most of the city is HOA-governed. City plan check requires an HOA approval letter describing scope, or an HOA-stamped plan set signed by an authorized officer. Form 220 (HOA Slope Landscape Management Notification) applies when work touches HOA-maintained slopes.

## How you submit

- Everything goes through the online Permit Center (Tyler EnerGov CSS) at cityoflagunaniguel.org/CSS. Register an account, apply, upload PDF plans, pay invoices, track status, request inspections. Public Works uses a separate eTRAKiT portal for encroachment/transportation permits.
- Review time standards (AB 2234 compliance posting): first submittal 10 business days or fewer, resubmittals 5 business days or fewer.
- Owner-builders may pull permits in California (sign the owner-builder declaration). Contractors must hold a CSLB license and a city business license.

## Code basis

- 2022 California Building Standards Code (CBC, CRC, CEC, CPC, CMC, CalGreen, Title 24 Part 6 energy) with local amendments adopted by Ordinance 2022-219. The 2025 code cycle takes effect statewide 1 Jan 2026; the city will have re-adopted with amendments [verify ordinance number].
- Climate Zone 6 for Title 24 energy. Seismic Design Category D/E typical. Expansive soils are common citywide; the addition handout requires footings a minimum of 24 inches deep with rebar.
- Zoning: Title 9 of the Municipal Code (Municode). Residential fence and wall rules are in the residential supplemental regulations (reported as section 9-1-35.2) [verify]; 9-1-45.2 covers nonresidential.
- Grading Manual and handouts 301/302 govern any earthwork, slope setbacks, and geotechnical requirements.

## What a complete residential submittal contains (Handout 103 and AB 2234 list)

- Residential building permit application with project description and valuation.
- Plans to scale (historically three paper sets; now PDF upload, [verify] whether paper is still accepted). Sheets: site plan, foundation plan, floor plan, roof/framing plan, exterior elevations, sections, structural details.
- Site plan must show property lines, dimensions from all structures to property lines, easements, top and toe of slopes, existing and proposed structures, north arrow, address and APN.
- Structural calculations, wet-signed or digitally signed by a California-licensed civil/structural engineer or architect, for anything beyond conventional light-frame construction.
- Title 24 energy compliance forms (CF1R) for conditioned additions.
- CalGreen residential checklist.
- Geotechnical/soils report when near slopes, for grading, or when the Building Official requests it.
- OCFA screening check form where applicable (sprinklers, wildland interface areas).
- WQMP/water quality forms where impervious area thresholds are hit.
- HOA approval letter or HOA-stamped plans.
- School district fee certificate before issuance (larger additions).
- Life-safety items the checklist calls out: egress windows in sleeping rooms, stair width/headroom/riser limits, smoke and CO alarms throughout the dwelling.

## Project-type specifics

### Fences and freestanding walls
- Building permit exempt only under 42 inches (Ordinance 2022-219). Anything 42 to 72 inches needs a building permit; 72 inches is the stated maximum with permit [verify]. This is stricter than the state default of 7 ft.
- Zoning height limits: reported 42 inches in front setback and street-side portions, 6 ft in side and rear setbacks [verify in 9-1-35.2]. Fences in the front setback may also need a Planning use permit.
- Corner lots: sight-distance triangle cutbacks apply.

### Retaining walls
- Separate building permit for all retaining walls; grading permit may also apply if they are part of earthwork.
- State exemption (walls under 4 ft from bottom of footing to top, no surcharge) likely applies, but the city handout 116-118 governs [verify whether the city lowered it].
- Engineered design with structural calcs expected above the threshold; soils report where on or near slopes. The Grading Manual requires civil, structural, geotechnical engineers and an engineering geologist to set slope setbacks, approved by the Building Official. Retaining walls may be used to reduce slope setbacks when approved.
- HOA slope notification (Form 220) when the wall affects HOA-maintained slopes.

### Decks
- No deck-specific handout; use 103. The city's exemption list names platforms, walks and driveways under 30 inches, not decks explicitly, so assume a permit is needed for any deck [verify].
- Expect: footing details to 24 inch minimum, ledger attachment detail, guard and stair details, lateral load connectors, and setback compliance on the site plan.

### Patio covers and pergolas
- Permit required for all patio covers, pergolas, louvered systems and trellises.
- Handout 111 limits: accessory to a single-family dwelling, open sides per CRC Appendix (now Appendix AH), minimum 6 ft 8 in floor to soffit, attached or freestanding details, footings, wind uplift.
- Pre-engineered aluminum systems are accepted with ICC-ES reports; site-built wood covers need framing details and often calcs.

### Room additions
- Full 103 checklist above. School fees, Title 24, CalGreen, possible OCFA review. Setbacks per zoning district; lot coverage and height per district. Second-story additions trigger privacy and design review in some HOAs and possibly the city's Objective Design Standards.

### ADUs / JADUs
- Form 218 checklist. Site plan must show setbacks and top or toe of slope; 4 ft setback from top or toe of any slope 2:1 or steeper and over 10 ft high.
- Pre-Approved ADU Program offers three pre-approved plan sets that shorten review.
- State law: 60-day ministerial review, 4 ft side/rear setbacks, impact fees waived under 750 sf.

## Fees (July 2025 to July 2026 schedules, unverified)

- Valuation-based plan check + permit fee. Lowest tier total about $122.54. The $100k to $500k tier reported at $887.25 plus $5.00 per $1,000 over $100,000.
- Add-ons: technology fee 2 to 3 percent of plan check and permit fees (sources conflict), general plan update fee, planning review fee, SMIP state seismic fee, CBSC state fee, CalGreen fee.
- Structural reinspection about $138.
- Impact fees collected at issuance for new units.

## Implications for the product

1. The jurisdiction rules layer for this city is mostly captured by about a dozen PDF handouts plus Title 9 of the municipal code. That is a tractable ingestion target.
2. The HOA letter is a hard gate that the product should prompt for early; most homeowners will not know this.
3. Expansive soils and slopes drive engineering here more than wall height does. The intake must ask about slopes within 15 ft of the work and HOA slope ownership, and route anything on a slope to an engineer with soils involvement.
4. Fence threshold (42 in) is a local amendment that differs from state code. This is exactly the kind of detail the rules database must encode per city, not per state.
5. Output format: the city wants a site plan with distances to property lines, scaled sheets, and calcs signed by a California licensee. A PDF set plus a separate signed calc package matches their portal.
